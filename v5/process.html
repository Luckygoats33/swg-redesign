How SWG works

Start with the review, not a menu of services.

Every engagement begins by understanding the business. The review identifies what is actually constrained, what order to address it, and what to build first.

The SWG Method

Five stages. Each one produces something concrete before the next one begins.

Diagnose
We review the business across ten dimensions to find where growth is actually constrained. This is not a questionnaire. It is a working session with access to your tools, data, and team context.
Sequence
Most companies have five to ten things they could improve. The question is which one to do first. We rank constraints by impact and dependency so the first build unlocks the next.
Scope
Each build is scoped with clear deliverables, a timeline, and a definition of done. No discovery phases that run indefinitely. No retainers without outcomes.
Build
We build the thing. Website, CRM setup, ad campaign, AI workflow, reporting dashboard, operational playbook. The build ships in working condition, not as a deck of recommendations.
Operate
After the build ships, we measure whether it worked. Some clients hand off operations to their team. Others ask SWG to run the system ongoing. Both paths are supported.

What the review examines

Ten areas that together determine where growth is constrained and where to begin.

Business stage

Revenue level, team size, and operational maturity shape what is realistic to build next.

Growth goal

What the company is trying to achieve in the next 6 to 12 months and whether the current setup supports it.

Offer clarity

Whether the product or service is positioned clearly enough that a stranger could understand and want it.

Customer path

How a prospect finds the company, evaluates the offer, and becomes a customer. Where the path breaks down.

Sales process

How leads are qualified, followed up with, and closed. Whether this depends on one person or runs as a system.

Team ownership

Who owns marketing, sales, operations, and technology. Where gaps or overlaps create bottlenecks.

Tech stack

The tools currently in use, how well they are integrated, and where data is siloed or duplicated.

Marketing

What channels are active, what is producing results, and what is running without clear measurement.

AI opportunities

Which manual processes could be automated, which data is available to train on, and what the realistic ROI looks like.

Reporting

Whether the company knows its numbers. Revenue, cost of acquisition, lifetime value, and pipeline visibility.

What you receive

Four deliverables. Each one is a working document, not a slide deck.

Constraint map

A diagram of how your current problems connect. Most companies discover that what felt like five separate issues is actually two root constraints with downstream effects.

Priority sequence

The order in which to address each constraint, ranked by impact and dependency. What to build first, what to defer, and why.

Build scope

A specific, scoped proposal for the first build. Deliverables, timeline, investment, and definition of done. Ready to approve or adjust.

Operating rhythm

A recommendation for how the company should run after the build ships. Meeting cadence, reporting structure, and ownership assignments.
