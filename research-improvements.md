# SWG Website: Research-Backed Improvements

Compiled from 2025-2026 B2B consulting web design research. Prioritized for SWG versions (v1-v5).

---

## Typography Improvements

**Use a serif + sans-serif pairing for premium authority** -- HIGH
- What: Pair a refined serif (e.g. Playfair Display, Libre Baskerville) for H1/H2 with a clean sans-serif (Inter, Outfit) for body. The 2026 consensus is serif headings + sans body = premium consulting standard.
- Why: Serif headings convey intellectual authority and editorial quality. Pentagram, Medium, and Kinfolk all use this pattern. Pure sans-serif (current Syne+Outfit) reads more startup than consultancy.
- Versions: v5 (already has Syne+Outfit -- swap Syne for a serif), v3/v4 if refreshed.

**Tighten heading letter-spacing and increase body line-height** -- MEDIUM
- What: Current v5 uses -0.03em letter-spacing on headings (good). Ensure body text line-height is 1.6-1.8 and paragraphs max 65-75 characters wide.
- Why: Optimal readability drives longer session duration. Stripe-level polish comes from disciplined spacing.
- Versions: All versions.

## Color & Visual Design

**Keep violet -- it is differentiated** -- HIGH
- What: The current violet (#7C3AED) accent is a strong strategic choice. In 2026, "defaulting to blue is the riskiest choice" as every consulting firm uses blue. Violet signals innovation while maintaining professionalism.
- Why: McKinsey (blue), BCG (green), Bain (red), Deloitte (green), Accenture (purple) -- violet/purple is the most premium-coded color in consulting. SWG already owns this space.
- Versions: All -- maintain violet as primary accent.

**Add warm stone neutrals and reduce pure black** -- MEDIUM
- What: Use zinc-tinted near-black (#0A0A0A, already in v5) instead of pure black. Add stone-family grays (#78716C, #A8A29E) for secondary text and UI chrome. These add "human, approachable quality" to professional services.
- Why: Pure black on white creates eye strain. Warm neutrals feel premium (Kinfolk, Apple).
- Versions: v5 already has this -- ensure v3/v4 match.

**Consider a dark-mode variant** -- LOW
- What: Dark-first design (#09090B base, #FAFAFA text) is now standard in premium tech/consulting. Offer as toggle or for specific landing pages.
- Why: Dark interfaces reduce cognitive load and feel more premium. Linear, Vercel, and Arc all use dark-first.
- Versions: Future enhancement for v5+.

## Layout & Structure

**Implement Problem-Solution-Proof page structure** -- HIGH
- What: Every service page should follow: (1) Name the pain, (2) Present the solution, (3) Prove it with case study metrics. Pre-emptively address objections: "Is this right for my industry?", "Worth the investment?", "Can you deliver?"
- Why: B2B buyers do 67-80% of research before contacting sales. The website IS the sales conversation.
- Versions: v3/v4/v5 (already have this structure -- tighten the proof layer with specific metrics).

**Use a 6-page consulting framework** -- HIGH
- What: Home, About, Services/Problems, Case Studies, Process, Assessment/Contact. Each page has a primary conversion goal, secondary goal, and fallback path for unready prospects.
- Why: Consulting Success data shows this is the optimal structure for client-generating sites. Navigation should reflect buyer intent, not org chart.
- Versions: v3/v4/v5 already have this -- validate each page has clear conversion goals.

**Add contextual social proof throughout, not in one section** -- MEDIUM
- What: Client logos on homepage, relevant case studies on service pages, testimonials near CTAs, security/credibility badges on assessment page. Each placement should be contextual to the surrounding content.
- Why: Trust is the conversion driver for high-ticket B2B. 76% of B2B buyers say site design is the critical factor in buying decisions.
- Versions: All versions.

## Conversion Optimization

**Replace "Contact Us" with specific-value CTAs** -- HIGH
- What: Use "Get Your Free Growth Audit" or "Book a 30-Minute Strategy Call" instead of generic "Contact Us" or "Get Started." Follow the 3Ps: Prominence (visible without scrolling), Promise (specific value), Proof (evidence nearby).
- Why: Specific CTAs outperform generic ones. One study showed action-specific CTA copy ("Book My Demo" vs "Submit") significantly lifted completion.
- Versions: All versions -- audit every CTA for specificity.

**Add a lead magnet / ungated value before the assessment** -- MEDIUM
- What: Offer a downloadable (growth checklist, self-diagnostic scorecard, industry report) that captures email before pushing to the full assessment. Pop-ups (delayed, not immediate) can boost email captures up to 400%.
- Why: Not every visitor is ready for a full assessment. A lead magnet captures mid-funnel prospects. The assessment itself is high-commitment.
- Versions: v5 -- add a lightweight lead capture before or alongside the assessment CTA.

**Embed calendar booking directly** -- MEDIUM
- What: Let prospects book directly into a calendar (Calendly, Cal.com) instead of submitting a form and waiting. Remove navigation from the booking page to eliminate distractions.
- Why: Frictionless scheduling converts better than form-then-follow-up. Reduces drop-off between interest and meeting.
- Versions: v5 assessment flow.

## Mobile Experience

**Ensure 44px minimum tap targets** -- HIGH
- What: All buttons, links, and interactive elements must be minimum 44x44px on mobile. Match keyboard type to input field (email keyboard for email, numeric for phone).
- Why: Mobile is 80%+ of traffic. Undersized tap targets are the #1 mobile UX failure.
- Versions: All versions -- audit with Chrome DevTools mobile view.

**Use logic-first, not mobile-first** -- MEDIUM
- What: Structure content so buyers find what they came for immediately. On mobile, this means: hero with clear value prop, one primary CTA above fold, collapsible navigation, progressive disclosure of detail.
- Why: 2026 trend: B2B buyers don't want to "explore" -- they want to find what they came for and move on.
- Versions: v5 primarily.

## SEO Best Practices

**Implement E-E-A-T signals on every page** -- HIGH
- What: Add author bios with credentials, link to LinkedIn profiles, include "Experience" signals (years in business, client count, specific outcomes). Use schema markup (Organization, LocalBusiness, FAQPage).
- Why: E-E-A-T is arguably the most decisive ranking factor in 2026. Google distinguishes AI-generated content from genuine expertise through these signals.
- Versions: v5 (already has Organization schema -- add more).

**Build a focused content corpus of 50-100 pages** -- MEDIUM
- What: Map content to the buyer journey: awareness (explainers, how-it-works), consideration (X-vs-Y, ROI explainers, playbooks), decision (pricing context, case studies, implementation guides), post-purchase (admin guides, success plans).
- Why: The "Corpus of Content" model (50-200 focused pages) delivers higher ROI than sprawling content. Plan content as if AI bots will summarize and recommend it.
- Versions: Future content strategy for v5.

**Optimize for AI search (GEO)** -- MEDIUM
- What: Structure content with clear Q&A patterns, use schema markup, write content that AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) can cite. Include specific facts, numbers, and named methodologies.
- Why: "Total Search Optimization" across Google, AI assistants, and social platforms is the 2026 SEO standard. Being citable by AI assistants is the new featured snippet.
- Versions: v5 content strategy.

## Assessment / Intake Form

**Use conversational multi-step format (one question per screen)** -- HIGH
- What: Replace traditional multi-field forms with a one-question-per-screen flow. Show progress ("Step 2 of 5"). Front-load easy questions (name, company) before harder ones (budget, timeline).
- Why: Conversational forms achieve 15-25% higher completion rates. A B2B company adding step labels saw completion jump from 32% to 41%.
- Versions: v5 assessment (already multi-step -- validate field ordering and progress indicators).

**Reduce to essential fields only** -- HIGH
- What: 3-field forms complete at ~60%, 10-field forms at ~30%. Cut to: name, email, company, primary challenge, budget range. Collect everything else in the discovery call.
- Why: Every additional field reduces completion. One company dropping from 9 to 4 fields saw completion jump from 28% to 51%.
- Versions: All assessment versions -- audit field count.

**Add helper text explaining "why we ask"** -- MEDIUM
- What: For sensitive fields (budget, revenue, employee count), add small helper text: "This helps us match you with the right engagement level." Use conditional logic to hide irrelevant follow-ups.
- Why: Suspicion kills conversion on sensitive fields. Explaining "why" neutralizes it.
- Versions: v5 assessment.

## Navigation & Information Architecture

**Organize by buyer need, not internal structure** -- HIGH
- What: Navigation labels should be: "Problems We Solve" (not "Services"), "Results" (not "Case Studies"), "How We Work" (not "Process"), "Start Here" (not "Contact"). Maximum 5-6 top-level items.
- Why: Best 2026 B2B sites "treat navigation for user intent, not a map of your org chart." Ruthlessly cut menu items.
- Versions: v3/v4/v5 -- already have good intent-based labels, refine further.

**Add sticky/persistent CTA in navigation** -- MEDIUM
- What: Keep a primary CTA button ("Book a Call" or "Take the Assessment") visible in the nav bar at all times, including on scroll.
- Why: Reduces friction -- the conversion path should never be more than one click away regardless of scroll position.
- Versions: v5 (likely already has this -- validate persistence on scroll and mobile).
