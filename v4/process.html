How SWG works

Start with the review, not a menu of services.

SWG begins every engagement by understanding the business. The service recommendation comes after we know what is actually blocking growth.

The SWG Method

Diagnose. Sequence.
Build. Operate.

Five stages. Each one builds on the last. Nothing is built until the business is understood.

01

Diagnose

Before any work begins, SWG conducts a structured review of the business. We examine the growth goal, customer path, team structure, tech stack, marketing, and operations.

The output is a clear picture of what is working, what is not, and where the real constraints live. Most companies have never had this done honestly.

02

Sequence

Not everything can be fixed at once. The review produces a priority sequence: what needs to happen first, what depends on what, and what can wait.

This prevents the common pattern of building the wrong thing first or spreading resources across too many fronts simultaneously.

03

Scope

Each priority gets a clear scope: what will be built, who is responsible, what the timeline looks like, and what success means. No ambiguity about deliverables.

Scope is documented before work begins. Both sides know exactly what is being built and why.

04

Build

Execution against the scoped plan. This might be a website, a CRM rebuild, an AI workflow, a marketing campaign, or an operating system for the team.

SWG builds in focused phases. Each phase produces a working output, not just a plan or a deck.

05

Operate

After the build, SWG can stay on to operate: managing campaigns, monitoring performance, training the team, and adjusting the system as the business evolves.

This is optional. Some companies take ownership immediately. Others want ongoing partnership. Both paths are supported.

Scope of the review

What the review examines

The review is not a checklist. It is a structured conversation about ten areas that determine whether a business is ready to grow.

Business stage

Where the company is today, how it got here, and what the next milestone looks like.

Growth goal

The specific revenue or operational target, with a realistic timeline and resource picture.

Offer clarity

Whether the product or service is positioned clearly enough for the market to understand and buy.

Customer path

How someone goes from stranger to customer, and where the journey leaks or stalls.

Sales process

How leads become revenue. Whether the process is repeatable, documented, and measurable.

Team ownership

Who owns what. Whether roles are clear and whether the team can execute without the founder.

Tech stack

The tools in use, how well they are connected, and where manual work fills the gaps.

Marketing

Channels, campaigns, content, and spend. What is compounding and what is just activity.

AI opportunities

Where automation or AI could create leverage, and whether the foundation supports it.

Reporting

Whether the business has visibility into what matters, and whether decisions are data-informed.

After the review

What you receive

The review produces four deliverables. Together, they give you a clear picture and a clear path forward.

Constraint map

A visual and written summary of what is blocking growth right now. Not a list of everything that could be better, but a focused identification of the constraints that matter most.

This is the foundation. Every recommendation flows from it.

Delivered as a written document with diagrams
Priority sequence

The order in which constraints should be addressed. What needs to happen first, what depends on what, and what can safely wait. This prevents the common mistake of solving the wrong problem first.

The sequence accounts for budget, team capacity, and business urgency.

Delivered as a sequenced roadmap
Build scope

For each priority, a clear scope of work: what will be built, what the investment looks like, who is responsible, and what the timeline is. No vague promises or open-ended retainers.

This is a decision document. You know exactly what you are buying before you buy it.

Delivered as scoped proposals per priority
Operating rhythm

A recommended cadence for reviews, reporting, and adjustments after the build is complete. This is how the work stays alive instead of collecting dust.

Whether SWG operates it or your team does, the rhythm keeps the system accountable.

Delivered as a recurring review framework
