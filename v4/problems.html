What brings companies to SWG

The problem is rarely just one service.

Companies usually come to SWG when growth is no longer a marketing problem alone. The real issue is almost always structural.

The Pattern

Your systems exist.
They just don't connect.

"We have all the tools. We just can't get them to talk to each other."

Paid media, CRM, website, AI, reporting, and team workflows — they all exist, but none of them share data, triggers, or outcomes. You are not missing tools. You are missing the connection between them.

Every disconnected system creates drag. The longer it goes, the more you are optimizing in isolation while the real problem compounds underneath.

Eight patterns we see repeatedly

Each one sounds like a service request. What is actually underneath tends to be more connected than it first appears.

We need the site to finally match the company.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

The website was built before the business matured. Now the company has outgrown the design, the messaging, or both. Visitors see a version of the business that no longer exists.

What SWG reviews

  • Brand positioning and messaging clarity
  • Visitor path from landing to conversion
  • Content architecture and page hierarchy
  • Technical performance and SEO foundation

What may need to be built

  • Full site redesign tied to current brand positioning
  • Conversion-focused page templates
  • Content strategy that reflects the business today

We are spending, but it is not converting.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

Paid media is running but the landing experience, offer clarity, or tracking is broken. The ads may be fine. The destination is the problem.

What SWG reviews

  • Ad-to-landing-page alignment
  • Conversion tracking and attribution accuracy
  • Offer positioning and price framing
  • Funnel dropout points

What may need to be built

  • Dedicated landing pages with clear conversion paths
  • Proper tracking infrastructure across platforms
  • Offer restructuring and A/B test framework

Our CRM is messy.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

Customer data lives in multiple places with no single source of truth. The team cannot segment, cannot automate, and cannot measure lifetime value with confidence.

What SWG reviews

  • Current CRM structure and data hygiene
  • Lead lifecycle stages and ownership rules
  • Integration points between tools
  • Reporting accuracy and segment clarity

What may need to be built

  • CRM cleanup and deduplication
  • Lifecycle automation flows
  • Unified customer view across platforms

We need AI automation.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

The company knows AI could help but does not know where to start. Often the manual processes are not documented well enough to automate. The foundation work comes first.

What SWG reviews

  • Current manual workflows and time costs
  • Data availability and quality for automation
  • Team readiness and adoption capacity
  • ROI potential by process area

What may need to be built

  • Process documentation and workflow mapping
  • Targeted AI integrations with clear ROI
  • Training and adoption plan for the team

The founder is still the operating system.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

Critical business functions run through one person. Sales, client delivery, vendor management, and strategy all bottleneck at the founder. Growth cannot happen without delegation infrastructure.

What SWG reviews

  • Founder time allocation and decision bottlenecks
  • Team structure and role clarity
  • SOPs and documented processes
  • Delegation readiness by function

What may need to be built

  • Operating playbooks for repeatable functions
  • Role definitions and accountability structure
  • Systems that run without the founder in the loop

We are active everywhere, but nothing compounds.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

The company is posting, emailing, advertising, and attending events but none of these efforts connect. There is activity without architecture. Energy without leverage.

What SWG reviews

  • Channel performance and attribution
  • Content and campaign coordination
  • Customer journey across touchpoints
  • Resource allocation versus return by channel

What may need to be built

  • Integrated marketing strategy with channel priorities
  • Content system that feeds multiple channels
  • Measurement framework that shows what compounds

The team is doing work by hand.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

Proposals, invoices, onboarding, reporting, and follow-ups are all manual. The team is busy but not productive. Growth adds labor, not leverage.

What SWG reviews

  • Repetitive task inventory and time costs
  • Tool usage and integration gaps
  • Handoff points where work stalls or duplicates
  • Automation opportunities ranked by impact

What may need to be built

  • Workflow automation for high-frequency tasks
  • Template systems for proposals, reports, onboarding
  • Connected tool stack that reduces manual data entry

The last tech build needs a rescue.

What it sounds like

What is usually underneath

A previous agency or contractor left the project incomplete, over budget, or built on the wrong foundation. The company needs someone to assess what is salvageable and what must be rebuilt.

What SWG reviews

  • Current build quality and technical debt
  • Gap between what was promised and delivered
  • Feasibility of repair versus rebuild
  • Vendor and dependency risks

What may need to be built

  • Technical audit with honest salvage assessment
  • Rebuild plan on a sound foundation
  • Vendor transition and knowledge transfer
